Frequently Asked Questions & User Guide

WelfareAI Knowledge Base & Frequently Asked Questions

Comprehensive operational guide covering AI stress modeling, explainability, data privacy, welfare triage, and support workflows for uniformed personnel and officers.

🤖 AI Model, Architecture & Predictive Analytics

Q: How does the AI model evaluate stress and where is it computed?

A: The platform runs a dual-layer architecture. When you submit a check-in, inputs are transmitted securely to our FastAPI Python ML microservice running on port 8000. A trained Random Forest Classifier (60 estimators, balanced class weights) evaluates multi-variable operational patterns against baseline statistical distributions to compute your composite welfare risk index (0–100%) and feature attribution breakdown.

Q: What are the 8 key operational stress factors evaluated?

A: The model evaluates: 1. Weekly Workload / Duty Hours, 2. Daily Sleep & Recovery Hours, 3. Subjective Work Pressure Rating (1–10), 4. Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10), 5. Social & Family Support Rating (1–10), 6. Work-Life Balance Rating (1–10), 7. Shift Continuity & High-Intensity Days, and 8. Longitudinal Trend Velocity Delta.

Q: Why does the system explain which factors contributed most to my score?

A: To eliminate "black box" AI decisions. In high-stakes uniformed forces environments, personnel and officers must understand the exact operational drivers behind every flag (e.g. "62h duty + 4.5h sleep drove 72% of the elevated risk index") so actionable adjustments can be made immediately.

Q: What is the difference between Low, Moderate, and High concern levels?

A: Low / Balanced (0–30%): Operational variables within healthy baseline tolerances. Standard self-care recommended.
Moderate Strain (31–70%): Noticeable workload or sleep deficit. Tailored pacing and proactive rest suggested.
High Concern (>70%): Compounding fatigue indicators requiring confidential welfare officer review and rest rotation.

📝 Stress Check-In & PSS-10 Questionnaire

Q: What is the PSS-10 and why is it used?

A: The Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10) is the globally validated 10-item psychometric instrument developed by Dr. Sheldon Cohen. It assesses how unpredictable, uncontrollable, and overloaded personnel feel during their recent operational duties. Items 4, 5, 7, and 8 use reverse scoring for psychometric validity.

Q: How frequently should I complete a stress check-in?

A: Standard recommended frequency is weekly or bi-weekly during routine deployments, or at the conclusion of intense operations, high-altitude patrols, or prolonged shift rotations.

Q: Can I edit my answers after submitting a check-in?

A: Each submitted check-in represents an immutable timestamped assessment in your historical trajectory. If you made an error, you can submit an updated check-in immediately; the platform automatically compares the latest two check-ins.

🔒 Privacy, Data Security & Ethical Charter

Q: Can my Commanding Officer see my check-in responses or use this against my career?

A: Strictly NO. WelfareAI operates under an absolute non-punitive doctrine. Disciplinary authorities, court-martial bodies, and promotion boards are barred by software RBAC and ethical charter from accessing individual check-ins. Individual responses are strictly confidential.

Q: Is WelfareAI a psychiatric or clinical diagnostic tool?

A: No. WelfareAI is a preventive, non-clinical decision-support tool. It does not diagnose psychiatric illness, prescribe medication, or replace qualified medical officers.

Q: How is my authentication and health data secured?

A: All passwords use Bcrypt salting (10 rounds). Sessions use signed HttpOnly JWT cookies with Strict SameSite protection, and every critical transaction is recorded in an append-only audit trail.

📈 Trajectory, 'What Changed?' & Recovery Journey

Q: What is the 'What Changed?' feature and how does it help?

A: The What Changed? module computes exact mathematical deltas between your current check-in and immediately preceding check-in (e.g., +1.5h sleep/night, -8 duty hours/week, -4 PSS points) to visually correlate rest interventions with stress index reductions.

Q: Why does the Recovery Journey show 'Human-in-the-Loop' and 'Follow-up' as Pending?

A: In live deployment, high-strain flags are queued for the Unit Welfare Officer to review confidentially and schedule a consultation. In this evaluation portal, you can click the "⚡ Simulate Officer Review & Follow-Up" button to immediately advance the 6-stage lifecycle!

Q: How do I export my historical trend records?

A: Navigate to the Longitudinal Trajectory page (`trends.html`) and click the "📥 Export CSV" button above the historical log table.

🩺 Welfare Officer Triage & Early-Warning Center

Q: How does the Early-Warning Center detect emerging strain?

A: It classifies signals into 4 dynamic quadrants: New Signals (first elevated flag), Rising Signals (risk increased ≥ +6%), Persistent Signals (elevated across ≥ 2 cycles), and Improving Signals (risk reduced ≤ -6%), enabling proactive support before burnout occurs.

Q: What role does the Unit Welfare Officer play?

A: Welfare Officers act as confidential human advocates. They review incoming welfare signals, conduct supportive one-on-one sessions, coordinate with unit leadership for rest pacing, and log intervention outcomes without punitive repercussions.
